HEADLINE ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Data collected:  NONE
Data shared:     NONE

In the Play Console Data safety form, answer:
- "Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types?"
  -> NO
That single "No" is the truthful answer for Dosique, because of how the
app is built (see WHY, below). The console will then show the listing
badge "No data collected" and "No data shared".

WHY EVERY COMMON DATA TYPE IS "NOT COLLECTED"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Play's definition of "collected" means data that is transmitted off the
device to the developer or a third party. Dosique transmits nothing:

- The app does not request the INTERNET permission. It has no server,
  no account system, no analytics SDK, no ads SDK, and no crash
  reporting. Code-wise it cannot send data out.
- Health info (medicines, dose history): entered by the user, stored
  only in the app's private on-device storage. Never transmitted.
  -> Health data: NOT collected, NOT shared.
- Personal info (profile names for self/family/pets, prescriber or
  pharmacy names the user types): on-device only. -> NOT collected.
- Photos (medicine photos the user picks): chosen through the Android
  system photo picker, copied into private on-device app storage.
  -> NOT collected, NOT shared.
- App activity / interactions: no analytics of any kind. -> NOT
  collected.
- Device or other IDs: the app reads and sends none. -> NOT collected.
- Location, contacts, calendar, audio, messages: the app does not
  access these at all (SMS/WhatsApp "sending" is done by handing text
  to the user's own messaging app through the Android share sheet —
  Dosique itself does not read or send messages). -> NOT collected.

User-initiated sharing is not "collection" or "sharing" in Play's
sense: when the user shares a schedule summary, a doctor-report PDF,
or a backup file, they pick the destination app themselves. That data
goes from the user's hand to the user's chosen app — the developer is
never a recipient.

SECURITY PRACTICES (console questions)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- "Is all of the user data collected by your app encrypted in transit?"
  -> Not applicable in substance: no data is transmitted at all.
  In the console, this question only applies if you declare collected
  data; with "No data collected" it is skipped / answered accordingly.
- "Do you provide a way for users to request that their data is
  deleted?"
  -> Users control deletion directly: any entry can be deleted in the
  app, and clearing app storage or uninstalling deletes everything.
  There is no server copy, so no request to the developer is needed.
  If the console asks for a data deletion URL/mechanism, point to the
  privacy policy (Section 5, "Deleting your data") at its public URL:
  [PRIVACY POLICY URL]

HEALTH DECLARATION (Play Console "Health content and services" /
health apps declaration)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Google Play requires apps in the Health & Fitness / Medical area to
complete a health declaration. For Dosique, the truthful position is:

- Dosique is a medication REMINDER and RECORD-KEEPING tool.
- It does NOT provide medical advice, diagnosis, treatment
  recommendations, dosage calculation, or drug-interaction checking.
- It is NOT a medical device and does not connect to any medical
  hardware or health platform (no Health Connect integration).
- All health-related information in the app is entered by the user and
  stays on the user's device.

So, in the declaration form: select the options that describe an app
which helps users track/record health information they enter
themselves and set reminders, and do NOT select anything claiming
diagnosis, treatment, disease management advice, interaction
checking, or medical-device functions. The exact wording and checkbox
labels of this form change from time to time — CONFIRM IN CONSOLE:
read each statement before ticking it, and only agree to statements
that match the paragraph above. If the form asks whether the app
provides health content/services "for medical purposes", answer in
line with the disclaimer in the listing and privacy policy: reminder
and recording tool only.
